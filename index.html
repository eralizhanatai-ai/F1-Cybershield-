Inside the car

Front wing

The first to meet the air

It guides airflow around the front wheels and towards the rest of the car. Downforce on the front axle helps the car turn.

Air becomes part of the design.

Inside the car

Tyres

An entire race on four contact patches

The tyres transfer acceleration, braking and cornering forces to the track. Grip changes with temperature, wear and the condition of the surface.

That is why CyberShield tracks four tyre temperatures, not just one.

Inside the car

Brakes

Turning speed into heat

Under braking, the discs and pads dissipate the energy of motion as heat. Brake and tyre temperatures are separate measurements.

Brake temperature is not yet simulated in this project.

Inside the car

Suspension

Keeping the wheels connected to the track

Suspension links connect the wheels to the chassis. They control wheel movement and the attitude of the car, affecting both grip and aerodynamics.

Mechanics and airflow work together.

Inside the car

Monocoque

A structure built around the driver

The central composite structure surrounds the driver. Other assemblies attach to it, and it acts as a survival cell in an accident.

The most important task is protecting a person.

Inside the car

Halo

Protection above the cockpit

The structure around the driver’s head helps protect against large objects and impacts. It is part of the car’s safety system.

A physical machine needs physical protection.

Inside the car

Steering wheel

A control panel in the driver’s hands

The driver does more than steer. A display, buttons and switches provide access to car settings and communication with the team.

Both engineers and drivers rely on data.

Inside the car

Sidepods

Airflow for cooling

The sides of the bodywork direct air towards the radiators and around the car. Cooling helps keep its systems at working temperatures.

A high temperature can be a real mechanical problem, not an attack.

Inside the car

Power unit

Turning energy into motion

A hybrid power unit combines a combustion engine, electrical systems and energy storage. Their performance depends on many operating parameters.

This project tracks a simplified engine temperature measurement.

Inside the car

Floor and diffuser

Downforce beneath the car

The floor shapes airflow beneath the car. Pressure differences help press it towards the track, while the diffuser manages the flow exiting at the rear.

Much of the work happens out of sight.

Inside the car

Rear wing

Balancing grip and drag

The rear wing creates downforce on the rear axle. Engineers balance cornering stability against aerodynamic drag on the straights.

Movable aerodynamic elements depend on the regulations for each season.

Inside the car

Sensors

The car tells its story

Sensors measure temperatures, pressures, speeds and loads. Telemetry carries those readings to the team so engineers can monitor the car.

Before trusting the numbers, check the message.

From physical parts to digital trust

Can this data
be trusted?

We have explored the machine. Now look at the messages it sends. What if a packet comes from an unknown source, has been altered, or is sent a second time?

F1 CyberShield is our educational answer. It simulates telemetry from two cars and checks packet structure, authorised sources, HMAC signatures and sequence numbers. Every message receives a clear result.
